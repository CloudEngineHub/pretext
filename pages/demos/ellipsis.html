Where the cut goes

Chrome, Safari and Firefox end a clamped box the same way. The last line breaks where it would without the clamp. If the ellipsis fits after it, it goes there; if not, the line is cut after the last character that leaves the ellipsis room, inside a word if need be. The ellipsis is drawn in the paragraph's first font. The demo does the same with layoutNextLine(): once per line at the full width, then for the last line again at the width less the ellipsis, which takes the words that fit, and once more from there, which cuts into the next word as overflow-wrap: break-word would.

Where the two columns differ, it is inside a word. Pretext knows how wide a word's letters are when the word is broken across two lines, not where each letter sits in the whole word, which is what a browser cuts by. In Latin text the cut lands one letter early at a rare width. In Arabic, whose letters change shape with their neighbours, Chrome and Firefox show one to three more letters than Pretext in two cuts out of three; narrow the Arabic paragraph to see it. A line that mixes directions differs too: browsers cut the end of the line as painted, and Pretext, which keeps no painted order, cuts the end of the text.

The line stream only walks forward, so the middle ellipsis measures each end it could keep as the line that starts there.
